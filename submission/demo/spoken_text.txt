Ghostline brings the walkie-talkie ritual into your browser. Hold to talk. Release to listen. It is built for small, temporary crews, with a familiar radio interface and no always-open microphone.

Choose a callsign, create a channel, and invite your crew with a code, a link, or a QR invitation generated on your device. Up to six radios can join. Channels expire after four hours.

The connection is real WebRTC audio. A Cloudflare Worker and D1 coordinate membership, signaling, and a single speaker at a time. Microphones start disabled. Releasing the button, leaving the page, or losing focus stops transmission. Each transmission is limited to fifteen seconds.

This local diagnostic uses two real application clients with synthetic tones. It verifies decoded audio in both directions and checks that releasing before a pending grant never opens the microphone. It does not replace a physical two-device test.

Kiro helped implement QR invitations and microphone selection using the project's steering and requirements. Code review exposed a microphone rollback bug, and the follow-up task corrected it. The repository records that work alongside Codex's implementation and verification.

Ghostline resurrects a simple idea: say what matters, then give the channel back.
